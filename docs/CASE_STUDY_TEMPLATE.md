## Owner bottlenecks before RelayOS

Describe the repeated questions, undocumented decisions, onboarding friction, owner interruptions, or knowledge-loss risk the client reported before the sprint. Distinguish the client’s qualitative account from any measured baseline.

## Source materials captured

List the source types organized during the engagement—such as job descriptions, SOPs, checklists, customer scripts, dispatch notes, policies, and owner interview evidence—without publishing confidential content.

## Approved knowledge created

Summarize the reviewed operational topics and the number of source-backed claims actually approved. Do not count drafts, rejected claims, unresolved conflicts, or interview answers as approved knowledge.

## Authority decisions clarified

Record the subjects where the client explicitly clarified what the employee may decide, what limit applies, and when owner approval begins. Do not imply that RelayOS independently determined authority.

## Escalations clarified

Record the triggers, destinations, required context, and urgency the owner approved for human handoff. Note any destinations or exception paths that remained unresolved.

## Employee questions handled

Report only actual structured questions exercised during the engagement. Separate cited answers, prohibited outcomes, withheld outcomes, and escalations. Omit raw sensitive question content.

## Gaps discovered

List genuine missing, incomplete, conflicting, provenance-invalid, unsupported, or unclear-authority records discovered through coverage or real questions. Do not describe known approval or escalation policy as a gap.

- After: [client-provided result, same measurement method and comparable period—or “not measured”]
- Interpretation limits: [confounders, sample size, and what cannot be claimed]

Never infer a productivity, savings, revenue, compliance, or employee-performance result from anecdotal use.

## Qualitative client quote

## Limitations

Document the engagement’s actual limits, including the one-company/one-role scope, source authenticity limits, any unreviewed topics, and whether work occurred in the current session-only static application or a controlled private delivery process. Do not claim production SaaS readiness.
